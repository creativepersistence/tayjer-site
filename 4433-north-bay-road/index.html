The Escalation

The boldest price in the history of real estate.

Every other listing on Earth begs. It sits, it stales, it cuts. This one climbs — publicly, mercilessly, on a fixed rule, whether you are ready or not. Owner Matthew Barnes, founder and chief executive of GO VIP Worldwide, set the terms on day one and has never blinked: half again as much, every business day, for as long as it takes.

It launched on 16 January 2026 at $7,500,000 — no luxury brokerage, no white-glove whisper campaign, just a flat-fee listing and total nerve. On 27 January, Barnes reset it to $11,200,000 and started the clock. From that moment the arithmetic did what a hundred glossy brochures could not: ×1.5, every business day, compounding, in front of the entire world.

It obliterated $165,000,000 — the highest advertised home price Guinness World Records has ever logged — inside two weeks. It smashed $288,000,000 the following week. And then something happened that has never happened to a house: the databases gave out. By 7 February the price had stopped being a number and become a stress test, with every major property system in America displaying a different ceiling, each one the largest figure it was physically capable of holding.

No home has ever been advertised for more. There is no second place.

The Residence

And the house more than earns it.

Forget the headlines for sixty seconds. What you are buying is a flawless piece of contemporary architecture, rebuilt to the studs, furnished to the last object, and ready to live in the day you get the keys.

Designed by the celebrated studio Domo and built in 2015 by Todd Michael Glaser — the developer behind some of the most consequential houses in Miami Beach — 4433 North Bay Road is two storeys of glass, stone and light on a gated, fully hedged lot. A private motor court. A pool and a separate lap pool wrapping the garden. Not one neighbour with a sightline in. Absolute, uninterrupted privacy in the middle of the most watched street in Florida.

Inside, it refuses to be ordinary. The ground floor is composed as four distinct rooms rather than one echoing open box: a soaring double-height family room anchored by a stone fireplace, a dining room, a chef's kitchen — and, almost unheard of at this scale, a dedicated 19′8″ × 22′10″ music room built for a grand piano. Upstairs, all three bedrooms are en suite. The main suite runs nearly thirty feet, with its own stone-clad bathroom, walk-in dressing room and private balcony over the garden.

And it is completely turn-key. When Barnes and his wife rebuilt it in 2025 they finished it as an acquisition, not a project: the bespoke furnishings stay. The rare design pieces stay. The art stays. You are not buying a house and then spending two years and seven figures making it liveable — you are buying a finished world, at $2,786 per square foot as it was last honestly priced.

It is not the biggest house on North Bay Road. It is the one everybody knows.
