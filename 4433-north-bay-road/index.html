The Escalation

A price with a schedule instead of an appraisal.

Most listings drop until someone bites. This one does the opposite, on a fixed rule, in public. Owner Matthew Barnes — founder and chief executive of GO VIP Worldwide — set the terms at the outset: the ask goes up half again as much every business day the house sits.

The house came to market on 16 January 2026 at $7,500,000, listed through the flat-fee service Beycome rather than a luxury brokerage. On 27 January, Barnes reset it to $11,200,000 and started the clock. From there the arithmetic did the marketing: ×1.5, every business day, compounding.

It cleared $165,000,000 — the highest advertised home price Guinness World Records has on file — inside two weeks. It cleared $288,000,000 the following week. By the time syndication caught up on 7 February, the number had stopped being a price and started being a stress test: different property databases displayed different ceilings, each one the largest integer that particular system could hold.

The Residence

Underneath the number is an actual house.

Built in 2015 by developer Todd Michael Glaser with architecture by Domo, 4433 North Bay Road is a two-storey contemporary set on an elevated bayfront site — a deliberate choice on a barrier island, and one that increasingly reads as infrastructure rather than styling.

Barnes and his wife took it down to the studs in 2025 and rebuilt it as a turn-key acquisition: bespoke furnishings, rare design pieces and the art stay with the house. Before the escalation began, that reimagined residence was brought to market at $9,900,000 — roughly $2,786 per square foot, which is what a serious conversation about this property has historically sounded like.

Three bedrooms, three and a half baths, 3,534 square feet on a 6,750 square foot lot. It is not the largest house on North Bay Road. It is, at the moment, the loudest.

The Street

North Bay Road, or: why any of this works.

The Wall Street Journal calls North Bay Road "Miami's Richest Road." The stretch holds an estimated $1.7 billion in property value across a single waterfront run, with the bay on one side and a very short list of owners on the other.

The immediate neighbours are the reason the stunt has an audience at all: Cindy Crawford; Shutterstock founder Jon Oringer; and David Beckham, whose estate a few doors down traded at roughly $72 million. In February 2026 the average list price on the road was about $25.2 million, up from $11.8 million a year earlier.

Put plainly — this is a street where a number nobody believes still gets read by everybody who matters.
